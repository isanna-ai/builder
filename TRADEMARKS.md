# Trademark policy

The **code** of isanna Builder is open source under Apache-2.0. The **name** is not. This is the
deliberate "code-open, name-protected" posture: Apache-2.0 §6 grants no trademark rights, and this
file says what that means in practice.

## The marks

"**isanna**", "**isanna Builder**", the isanna wordmark, and the aqua droplet logo are trademarks of
the project maintainer.

## What you can do without asking

- Use the software for anything the Apache-2.0 licence allows.
- Fork it, modify it, redistribute it — under a **different name**.
- State factually that your product "is built on isanna Builder" or "is compatible with isanna
  Builder", as long as you don't imply endorsement or official status.
- Use the marks in prose to refer to this project (e.g. in an article, a talk, a comparison table).

## What needs permission

- Naming a fork, a distribution, a hosted service, or a competing product "isanna" or "isanna
  Builder" (or a confusingly similar name).
- Using the logo or wordmark as the identity of *your* product, service, or company.
- Anything that suggests your offering is the official isanna Builder, or is endorsed by or
  affiliated with the project, when it is not.

## Why

A verification tool's whole value is that its verdict can be trusted. If anyone could ship "isanna
Builder" with the gates quietly disabled, the name would stop meaning "the host ran the tests" — the
one thing it exists to guarantee. Keeping the name protected keeps the guarantee honest, while the
code stays free for everyone to use, audit, and build on.
